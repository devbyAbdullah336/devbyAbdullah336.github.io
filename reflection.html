Final Reflection

Looking back at 13 weeks of development on SoulsArena

Looking back at Week 1 when I just had a basic character jumping around on a blank layout, finishing SoulsArena has been an incredible experience. This capstone project was the biggest and most complicated programming challenge I’ve ever taken on, and it completely changed how I think about logic, problem-solving, and software development.

What I Learned About Programming

The biggest technical lesson I learned is that making code work is only half the battle; building systems that don't break when you add *new* mechanics is where the real skill comes in. When I began linking complex nodes like motion warping, custom hitbox variables, and round logic, old functions would constantly break.

Spending hours tracking down blueprints taught me how to stop getting overwhelmed and start breaking massive technical bugs down into tiny, actionable components—like spending 30 minutes tracing a single node path until hit detection worked flawlessly.

Core Project Takeaways

  • Scope Control: It is incredibly easy to get carried away wanting to add a million features at once. Through mentor feedback from Ms. Johal, I realized that focusing heavily on deep polish (like smooth hit reactions and solid combat mechanics) is infinitely better than building a massive, broken environment.
  • Advanced AI Architectures: Coding enemy behavior trees so they don't simply swarm the player simultaneously, but instead track their surroundings, maintain spacing, and take turns attacking was a huge step up for me. It forced me to think deeply about gameplay balance and user experience.
  • Full-Stack Integration: Building the front end game systems alongside this structured website portfolio helped me realize how different engineering platforms connect together in professional workspaces.

Looking Ahead

This capstone project proved to me that I can take a complicated idea directly from my head, design it entirely from absolute zero, push through the frustration of endless bug-fixing cycles, and deliver a fully functional build. Wrapping up this project has completely solidified my plans to pursue Computer Science at university and build a real career in game engineering and software architecture.
